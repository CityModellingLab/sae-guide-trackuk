Direct estimation

Survey design-based estimates and uncertainty

Theory in brief

For an area-time cell (j,t), the target is a population mean or proportion:

\mu_{jt} = \frac{1}{N_{jt}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{jt}} y_{ijt}.

If sampled unit i has inclusion probability \pi_{ijt} and design weight w_{ijt}=1/\pi_{ijt}, the Horvitz–Thompson ratio estimator gives the design-weighted direct estimate:

\hat{\mu}^{w}_{jt} = \frac{\sum_{i \in S_{jt}} w_{ijt} y_{ijt}} {\sum_{i \in S_{jt}} w_{ijt}}.

Estimation approach

Target estimand
  • Outcome and Population: Share of adults who cycle for transport frequently
  • Geography: MSOAs in Wales
  • Time: 2021, 2023, and 2025

Direct estimation is the starting point for the SAE workflow. Each area-time estimate, or MSOA-year cell in this example, is calculated only from the survey respondents observed in that area and wave. For each MSOA-year cell, we calculate a Horvitz–Thompson weighted proportion using the respondent cycling outcome, matched survey weight, survey stratum, MSOA assignment, and wave year.

The output is a transparent survey-based baseline: a direct estimate, sampling variance, and usable sample size for each observed MSOA-year cell. These estimates show where the survey evidence is strong, sparse, missing, or unstable, helping us decide whether model-based small area estimation is needed.

1 Prepare survey data

We use a Horvitz–Thompson weighted proportion among usable adult respondents. The cleaned survey microdata input has one row per respondent-wave for the cycling estimand. After filtering, each retained row has a valid binary cycling response, matched weight, MSOA assignment, wave label, and survey stratum:

Column Role

2 Calculate direct estimates

svyby() computes the weighted share and uncertainty diagnostics for every area-time cell with usable observations. The completed area-by-time grid retains missing cells for later modelling.

# Define survey design
bike_design <- svydesign(
  ids = as.formula("~1"),
  strata = as.formula("~strata"),
  weights = as.formula("~weight"),
  data = survey_bike
)

# Calculate direct estimate and uncertainty
direct_bike_estimates <- svyby(
  formula = as.formula("~freq_biker"),
  by = as.formula("~area_id + time_id"),
  design = bike_design,
  FUN = svymean,
  vartype = "se",
  na.rm = TRUE,
  keep.names = FALSE
) |>
  as_tibble() |>
  transmute(
    area_id,
    time_id,
    direct_estimate = freq_biker,
    variance = se^2
  )

# Calculate sample size per area-time cell
direct_bike_counts <- survey_bike |>
  group_by(across(all_of(c("area_id", "time_id")))) |>
  summarise(n = n(), .groups = "drop")

# Join together
direct_bike <- domain_frame |>
  left_join(direct_bike_estimates, by = c("area_id", "time_id")) |>
  left_join(direct_bike_counts, by = c("area_id", "time_id")) |>
  mutate(n = replace_na(n, 0L))

3 Assess precision and coverage

Direct estimation provides a useful starting point because it makes three limitations visible:

  • Uneven temporal and spatial coverage: The number of usable observations varies across survey waves and MSOAs, leaving some area-time cells with much less direct evidence than others.
  • Unstable area estimates over time: For some MSOAs, direct estimates change sharply between waves, which may reflect real behavioural change or sampling noise.
  • Zero within-area variance: For binary indicators, small samples can contain only one response value, producing zero or unrealistically low variances.

Sparse and uneven survey coverage limits how far direct estimates can be used on their own. At the target MSOA-year scale, many estimates are unstable or unavailable, so the next step is to use model-based SAE methods that borrow strength across areas, years, and auxiliary predictors.

Two model-based options follow:

  • Area-level SAE models stabilise direct area estimates using sampling variances to control how much weight each direct estimate receives.
  • Unit-level SAE models estimate the individual outcome from survey microdata and aggregate predictions to target area-years.
Code 
direct_summary_by_wave <- direct_bike |>
  group_by(time_id) |>
  summarise(
    `Usable observations` = sum(n),
    `MSOAs without direct estimates` = sum(is.na(direct_estimate)),
    `MSOAs without variance` = sum(is.na(variance)),
    `MSOAs with zero variance` = sum(variance == 0, na.rm = TRUE),
    `Median sample size` = median(n),
    .groups = "drop"
  ) |>
  rename(`Wave` = all_of("time_id"))

The choice between area-level and unit-level modelling should depend on two things: the quality of the direct estimates as model inputs, and whether the survey microdata contain unit-level predictors that help explain individual variation in the indicator.

In this setting, the summary points to important limitations for area-level modelling. Because the outcome is binary, many MSOA-year cells contain limited direct survey information: small binary samples often produce all-zero or all-one responses. This motivates prioritising unit-level modelling, where respondent characteristics can be used to model the outcome before predictions are aggregated back to areas. We still fit an area-level model first to show how much stabilisation is possible from direct-estimate inputs before moving to unit-level modelling.
