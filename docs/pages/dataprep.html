Modelling Data Preparation

This workbook introduces the survey source, defines the estimand, documents the synthetic geography, and explains the analysis-ready data products used by the estimation workflows that follow.

1 Synthetic National Survey for Wales response datasets - 3 waves

The tutorial uses a synthetic teaching dataset based on the National Survey for Wales (NSW). The NSW is a repeated cross-sectional survey of people in Wales, with annual survey periods covering topics such as transport, health, education, public services, housing, internet access, climate change, and the Welsh language. For this tutorial, the cleaned microdata combine three survey periods: 2020-2021, 2022-2023, and 2024-2025 into one harmonised survey microdata.

Since the survey responses are geo-indexed at the Local Authority level, but the practical tutorial estimates indicators at MSOA level, we use a synthetic MSOA assignment. The cleaned survey file keeps each respondent’s Local Authority and assigns a synthetic MSOA within that Local Authority using deterministic random allocation. As such, estimates produced from these data should not be read as substantive findings about individual MSOAs in Wales.

This design has two purposes:

  • Higher spatial resolution for tutorial purposes. The tutorial can demonstrate MSOA-level direct estimation, area-level SAE, spatial modelling, and MRP-style poststratification.
  • Privacy and disclosure protection. The assigned MSOA is not the respondent’s true MSOA, so the tutorial avoids presenting fine-grained respondent locations.

2 Defining the Estimand

It is important to define the estimand before proceeding to the next step. The estimand determines how we can formulate the appropraite indicator from survey variables, how we aggregate it to the desired area level, and which model inputs such as predictors and spatio-temporal structure are valid downstream. A well-formed estimand answers:

Estimate the measure of what outcome, among which population, across which geography and time period.

For the main tutorial, we use seek to perform SAE on estimand:

Component Choice
Measure Share
Outcome Walks for transport daily
Target population Adults
Geography MSOAs in Wales
Time 2021, 2023, 2025

The target population definition is particularly important when we are estimating means or proportions since it determines the denominator for such aggregation. Moreover, it dictates the requirement for target population sizes or poststratification tables when formulating the models.

3 Preparing the Survey Indicator for the estimand

Since we are interested in whether our respondents walk frequently as the main mode of transport, we can code the indicator from AtFrqWlk10, the active-travel frequency question for walking.

Field Definition
Indicator name freq_walker
Raw survey variable AtFrqWlk10
Positive case AtFrqWlk10 == 1, walking daily
Negative case Walking but not daily
Missing Missing, invalid, no matched analysis weight, or not asked
Measure Share
Target population Adults

Since the survey question was administered to all respondents during some survey periods and subsampled in others, the corresponding weight variable selection rule is as follows: - Use SampleTravelWeight in survey periods where this question was asked to a subsample and the weight for this exists, - Use SampleAdultWeight in survey periods where this question was asked to the main sample. The matched analysis weight is stored as weight variable.

The current cleaned data give the following usable observations for the walking estimand:

Year Usable walking observations MSOAs with usable observations
2021 3,450 408
2023 1,999 406
2025 5,893 408

Note that although the data have gaps when aggregated to the area-year level, that is expected. SAE models will be used to borrow strength for sparse or unsampled domains.

4 Preparing auxiliary data

4.1 Area Predictors

Area-level SAE needs covariates that plausibly explain spatial differences in daily walking. The area predictors drawn from Census 2021 bulk tables at MSOA level. The table keeps raw variables for interpretation and z-scored cov_* variables for modelling.

These variables are chosen because daily walking is likely to vary with density, car access, commuting mode, housing form, and the socioeconomic and student composition of each area.

Variables Why include
Population density Denser urban areas tend to be more walkable
Share of households without car/van Individual no-car households tend to walk, cycle, take public transport more
Working from home, Full-time students and routine/semi-routine occupations Individual occupation may explain their propensity to walk

4.2 Poststratification Table

A population poststratification table is needed to apply the fitted unit-level model to the target population. Because the estimand is the share of adults who walk for transport daily, the tutorial uses an MSOA-by-year-by-age-by-sex population table.

We built our poststratification tables from ONS MSOA quinary age-by-sex population estimates and keeps the survey years used in this tutorial: 2021, 2023, and 2025. A few notes on the preparation of the poststratification tables:

  • The youngest available ONS age band is 15 to 19. This is used as the closest available population cell for the 16+ walking estimand.
  • For MRP, the variables that define poststratification cells must be available in both the survey and the population table, with compatible categories.

Note that other useful predictors can still enter the unit-level model as area-level predictors. For example, population density and no-car households are joined by MSOA. They help explain spatial differences in walking, but they do not need to define additional poststratification cells.

Column Meaning
domain_id MSOA estimation area
wave_year Tutorial survey year
age_band Quinary age band used in the unit-level model
sex Sex category used in the unit-level model
pop_count Number of adults in the cell
